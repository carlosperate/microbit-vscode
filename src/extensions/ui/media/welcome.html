Welcome to the BBC micro:bit IDE

Write code, send it to a BBC micro:bit, and watch it run. MicroPython or C++ projects, all in the browser.

Five IDE features to explore
1Try your program in the simulator

No micro:bit to hand? In MicroPython mode the simulator in the sidebar runs your program in the browser. Press its buttons, shake it or change the temperature, and watch the display react. C++ programs need a real board.

2Send it to the micro:bit

Plug the micro:bit in over USB and press Build & flash. It uses WebUSB, so you need a Chrome-based browser such as Chrome or Edge. Pair once, when asked, and every flash after that goes straight through.

3Connect to serial

Whatever your program prints, and every error it hits, comes back from the micro:bit over the serial terminal. With MicroPython it is a prompt too: press Ctrl+C to stop the program, then type Python a line at a time.

4Keep your work

Where your files live decides how long they last. Temporary storage is cleared when you close the tab. Browser storage keeps them in this browser, and the IDE reopens them on your next visit, with your files coming along when you switch to it. A local folder holds real files on your computer, in Chrome or Edge.

5Select your theme

Each mode brings its own colours. Any of the six micro:bit themes can replace them, three light and three dark. In temporary storage the choice lasts until you close the tab.
